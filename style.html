BEHIND THE SITE / HOUSE STYLE

How we write.
On purpose.

Every page here is written to one style, and the build fails if a page breaks it. This is the style: the rules, the banned constructions, and the gate that checks both.

The rules every page follows.

  • Second person, short declarative sentences, one idea per paragraph; a checklist when the reader has to do something.Basis: Google Search Central: creating helpful, reliable, people-first content
  • Every product statement names its listing and the date it was read.Basis: Google: write high quality reviews
  • Every guide ends with what it did not establish.Basis: Google: write high quality reviews
  • No experience we do not have: no invented tester, no hands-on claim, no quote without a source.Basis: FTC: Disclosures 101
  • No number without a dated first-party source behind it; absent numbers stay absent.Basis: Amazon Associates Operating Agreement
  • No urgency, countdown or scarcity framing; a seasonal page states calendar facts, never demand.Basis: Amazon Associates Program Policies

These were decided once, in the voice decision recorded on the decision desk, and written into the design brief. This page publishes them; the gate enforces them.

Constructions this site does not use.

One register-owned list drives this page and the release gate, so the two cannot drift apart. Each entry carries the reason it is banned and the official guidance behind the ban. The “AI slop” feel of a generated site comes from repeatable tells like these; the fix is to stop them at build time, not to promise an editor will catch them.

The gate runs on every build.

tools/check_publication.py scans every page this site renders for the banned constructions and fails the release when one appears in strict-scope visible text. Two kinds of text are exempt, on purpose: text whose job is to quote the rules — the phrase cells on this page and any element marked data-lint="quote" — and suggestions/index.html, The decision register quotes the banned constructions when it reviews them, and quotes Google's own warning wording; text marked data-lint="quote" anywhere, and the style page's phrase cells (class p26-banned-phrase), are exempt for the same reason.

The lint is a floor, not an editor. A sentence can pass it and still be badly written, which is what the review pass is for. Before the list shipped, every current reader page was scanned against it on 19 Nov 2026; buyer-facing copy was already clean, two meta-uses of banned tokens were rewritten rather than exempted — the front-page hero line and the about page’s must-have list phrase — and the scan and rewrites are recorded in sessions/3d0d7bd1/decisions.md.

Who may add a phrase to this list, or remove one, and on what evidence, is open question D3B-Q1 on the decision desk. Until it is answered, the safe default applies: the list grows when shipped copy would have failed it, and removals need a dated, recorded decision.
