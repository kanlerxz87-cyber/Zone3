The tightest constraint in the whole programme

Star ratings and customer reviews may not be displayed "in part or in whole" unless the link to them was obtained through Creators API or PA API under the License. Independently, Amazon's Community Guidelines — incorporated into the Operating Agreement — prohibit customer reviews created, edited or removed in exchange for compensation. For an LLM-driven platform those two clauses together rule out the two most tempting shortcuts: republishing review text, and generating reviews. Both are out of scope here by design.

Why aggregate-only is also the better product

The restriction pushes the design toward something genuinely more useful: an aggregate score with an "as of" stamp and a link to the live listing, plus a review-velocity signal for ranking. Readers get an honest summary and one click to the source of truth, instead of a page of cherry-picked quotes that went stale the day after publication.

It also survives the attribution rules. Review bodies cached on-site become inaccurate as soon as Amazon removes them, and the 24-hour cache ceiling means any textual design needs a refresh loop for no revenue benefit. Aggregates re-fetched at render time are cheap; quotes are not.
