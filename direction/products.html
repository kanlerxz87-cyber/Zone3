Why the two fields are never merged

The brief asks for an intended ASIN, an official listing URL, a confirmation date and a source, with observed redirect targets kept in a separate field. The reason is practical rather than procedural. An ASIN is a checkable identifier: if the intended ASIN and the observed destination are the same column, a later reader cannot tell whether a value was confirmed or merely observed, and a redirect that Amazon changes silently rewrites the record. Kept apart, the record can say the honest thing: we know where this link goes today; we do not yet know where it was meant to go.

What this record does not contain

  • No price, and no price comparison. Those values can only be published from Amazon-served content or the Creators API, and neither is available here.
  • No star rating, review count, sales rank or demand badge, for the same reason.
  • No claim that any outbound link is a validated Special Link. None has been tested in an enrolled Associates account. See 5B-Q05.
  • No assertion that the supplied short links are invalid. Their exact compliance status is unresolved and is recorded as unresolved in 5B-Q06.
