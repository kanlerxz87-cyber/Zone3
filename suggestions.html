Three reviewable design paths

DP-C is the recommended default for the “one site to index” requirement. DP-A is the visual pass; DP-B is the editorial operating rhythm. These can be combined, but none changes the live pages by itself.

DP-A Shopfront polish first

Thesis. Keep the existing root publication and make the next design pass buyer-led, not process-led.

  1. Lead with the two category choices, then show the five observed listing cards with clear status; do not imply intent or superiority.
  2. Keep the four question-led guides visible as editorial choices, and distinguish working hypotheses from measured queries.
  3. Group audit, decisions, sources and flagged records under one “Research & governance” menu item and repeat the group in the footer.
  4. Do not move or soften the disclosure strip; keep it under the masthead on every page and repeat it in the footer.

Resolves: The product-first front page, grouped navigation and disclosure requirements. · Effort: Small-to-medium; mostly template and layout edits, no new product data. · Risk: Low if the current generator/publish passes remain authoritative.

Do not: Do not add price/rating/review badges, untested verdicts, “trending” claims or generated product photography.

DP-B Magazine desk and measured cadence

Thesis. Make the site feel published through a real editor, a review gate and a steady question-led calendar—not through filler.

  1. Use one question per guide and cite every factual answer to an official source.
  2. Adopt one guide every 14 days only after a human reviewer is named; this interval is a proposed setting, not an owner decision.
  3. After Search Console is connected, choose the next question from dated query reports; until then use the supplied titles as hypotheses, not popularity claims.
  4. Add each accepted guide through the existing register-driven feed/sitemap build.
  5. Use a named byline only when the named person has actually reviewed the article; never create a fictional desk author.

Resolves: Authorship, cadence, question-shaped topics, organic measurement and the anti-slop request. · Effort: Ongoing editorial operations; the feed/sitemap parts are already automated. · Risk: Medium if the human gate is skipped or dates are left stale.

Do not: Do not use “trending”, “best”, “we tested” or seasonal urgency without appropriate dated evidence.

DP-C Consolidate the reader architecture before more content

Thesis. One public publication with clear categories; no parallel brand or near-duplicate page set.

  1. Treat root /Zone3/ as the recommended front door; retain /shop/ category pages and /guides/ as distinct same-site destinations.
  2. Inventory every unique claim and internal link in /site/, /read/, /marketing/ and /articles/ before deciding whether to merge, archive or redirect.
  3. Keep audit, sources, decisions and flagged items reachable from one grouped nav and the footer, not in the buyer hero.
  4. After a route map is approved, rebuild sitemap/feed and crawl all internal links; do not apply noindex/redirect rules speculatively.

Resolves: The “one site to index” request, category clarity and duplicated-brand problem. · Effort: Largest path; needs a single integration pass and a before/after link inventory. · Risk: Medium because route changes can strand links or discard unique evidence.

Do not: Do not delete, redirect or noindex an existing page until its unique content and inbound links are mapped.

Privacy and deployment gates

  • The checked-in browser rules write no cookies or browser storage and generate/send no visitor identifier.
  • The prototype suppresses sends when globalPrivacyControl is true or Do Not Track is 1; with enabled=false or an empty endpoint URL, it sends nothing.
  • The proposed payload omits user agent, referrer and query strings; do not call raw page views sessions or clicks conversions.
  • Keep sponsored Amazon anchors direct; do not add a redirect or change the href to measure a click.
  • Do not activate any runtime until its official logging/retention documentation and actual network/access logs, IP handling, retention, access controls and applicable notice/consent requirements have been reviewed.
  • Use authorized Associates first-party reports for order/conversion figures; do not create a browser checkout event or forecast.
