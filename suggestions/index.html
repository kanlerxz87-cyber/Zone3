THE NEXT DESIGN BRIEF

A prompt worth building from.

Each block does one job. Paste the whole brief into a design tool or hand a designer one block at a time. Every block is grounded either in a rule already recorded in this repository or in an official policy page, and none of them asks you to assert a fact the site does not have. It is a proposed direction grounded in the limits above — not an instruction to treat missing evidence as confirmed.

1 · The job of every page

A reader arrives with a question about a thing on their counter. The page answers four things in this order: what the linked listing actually says, what it does not say, what the reader should check at home, and where the sponsored link goes. Anything unverified is left visibly absent, with the gap named. A page that cannot answer all four is a note, not a guide.

Basis: Google: write high quality reviews

2 · Voice, and the words this site does not use

Second person, plain verbs, short sentences, one idea each. No “must-have”, “game-changer”, “obsessed”, “top-rated”, “we loved”, “In conclusion”, no countdowns, no invented urgency, no fake expert, no first-person experience of a product nobody used. Every product statement names the listing it came from, and the date it was read. The read-aloud test is the editor: a sentence you would not say at a dinner table gets rewritten.

Basis: FTC: Disclosures 101

3 · Architecture, top to bottom

Masthead → non-dismissible disclosure strip immediately under it → one editorial promise (not a value proposition) → the shortlist, filterable, cards carrying format illustration and the listing’s own words → a question router → the guide index → one seasonal cluster → a comparison table whose unverified cells are blank and labelled → footer that keeps the whole research set reachable in a single group. Product first, verification process second, always.

Basis: Amazon Associates Program Policies

4 · Visual system

Ivory canvas, deep green ink, one warm accent, hairline rules, generous rhythm. Serif display at 46–79px with tight leading, sans body at 15–17px with 1.8–1.95 line-height. Minimum 44px tap targets, visible focus ring, tables that scroll inside a labelled region. No gradient headline, no glass, no shadow soup, no card grid without a reason. Art is diagrammatic and generic, never a stand-in for a photograph of the SKU.

Basis: Amazon Associates Operating Agreement

5 · What pictures are allowed here

Two kinds only: a labelled AI editorial still life, and original format diagrams drawn for this site. No Amazon Program Content asset is downloaded or stored; no scraped photography; no image that implies a test result, a meal we cooked, or the exact look of a listed model. If real product photography ever appears, it is ours, dated, and captioned with who shot it.

Basis: Amazon Associates Operating Agreement

6 · Disclosure, links and the things that must never be improvised

The Associates statement appears under the masthead on every page and again in the footer. Every Amazon link carries rel="sponsored" and names its destination in the CTA copy. The tag-parameter requirement, the no-bookmarking rule and the no-association-of-sub-tags-with-users rule are treated as design constraints, not fine print. Prices and availability appear only where Amazon serves them or an API licence covers them. Any mention of a limited-time promotion inherits the duty to remove it when the promotion ends.

Basis: Amazon Associates Program Policies

7 · The editorial system

One question per page, question-shaped titles, a short-answer box at the top, three body sections that each carry their source links, an explicit “what we have not established” block, and related reads that go somewhere a reader actually needs. Cadence: review one guide every 14 days; publish only through the gate. Keep the ladder honest — published, buildable from the listings already read, and held. A held piece stays held; softening the gate to ship it is how a publication becomes a content farm.

Basis: Google: write high quality reviews

8 · Topics that fit this shelf

Write the buyer question the listing leaves open: what “Sync Finish” and “Hi-Fry” actually name, how quart counts mislead, whether two small baskets beat one big one, what a preset does when you press it, how to know a cook is done, what cleanup costs you in counter space, and how a probe changes a roast. Trending framing is allowed only as dated context (“it is hosting season”), never as a popularity claim, and never as a reason to invent a search-volume number.

Basis: Google: write high quality reviews

9 · Measurement without a surveillance build

Five first-party events, aggregate by UTC day, no cookies, no identifiers, no fingerprints, honouring Global Privacy Control, Do Not Track and Save-Data, and staying off until a durable host exists. A verified Search Console property and dated first-party reports are the only inputs that pick the next topic. A click is not an order; an order is not a trend.
