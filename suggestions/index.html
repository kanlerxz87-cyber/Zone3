Seven ways to make the shop feel more published — and what each one needs.

Review mode only, again. A dated review round, kept separate from the six standing proposals above. Every card is review-only: choosing an option saves a note in this browser and implements nothing. Each suggestion carries the sentence it would add to the design prompt and what evidence would change the decision. Each card joins the same local save/export record as the proposals above; nothing here is implemented by reading, choosing or saving it.
Ready — needs approval only · Editorial designD9-01

Ship a dated seasonal strip, not a hype banner

Late November through December is when the thermometer guides and the hosting checklist are naturally useful, and a calendar fact needs no demand claim. A restrained strip that says what today is and links the three guides that answer it makes the site feel tended rather than generated.

Adds to the design prompt: “Carry one restrained, dated seasonal strip that connects existing guides to the current cooking season; it states calendar facts only, never demand, popularity or trend claims.”

What would change our mind: If the strip dates badly or its guides draw no measurable engagement after the season, retire it — a stale strip is worse than none.

Basis: FoodSafety.gov: safe minimum internal temperatures

Ready — needs approval only · EditorialD9-02

Approve the article slate below, one guide at a time

The reading room grows credibility by rhythm, not volume: one new question-shaped guide at a time, each through the same publishing gate, each linked from two existing guides so the cluster stays one hop wide.

Adds to the design prompt: “Grow the reading room one question-shaped guide at a time; every new guide keeps the same evidence limits, related-links mesh, schema, canonical URL, sitemap and RSS entry as the current six.”

What would change our mind: A drafted guide that cannot cite its sources, or that repeats a question the room already answers, does not ship.

Basis: Google: write high quality reviews

Proposed — needs sourced Q&A pairs first · FindabilityD9-03

Add FAQPage structured data only where every answer is already sourced and visible

Article schema ships today. An FAQ block earns rich results only when the answers are actually on the page, and Google is explicit that invisible or empty-page markup is out of bounds — so the constraint is the editor, not the schema.

Adds to the design prompt: “Add a short FAQ block to a guide only when every answer is already stated and sourced in that guide, and mark it up with FAQPage structured data; never mark up content the page does not show.”

What would change our mind: If an answer needs a fact the register cannot source, the question is dropped, not inferred.

Basis: Google Search Central: introduction to structured data

Proposed — needs a layout audit first · Mobile and page experienceD9-04

Port the mobile pass into the reader template: type scale, tap targets, a sticky way to reach the comparison

The research layer already ships a clamped type scale, 44-pixel tap targets and a sticky quick-compare bar; the reader template should carry the same discipline so long guides keep the comparison one thumb-reach away.

Adds to the design prompt: “Keep a mobile-first type scale, 44-pixel minimum tap targets, and a sticky way to reach the format comparison from long guides.”

What would change our mind: If the sticky bar overlaps content or traps scrolling on any viewport, simplify it rather than patch it.

Basis: Google Search Central: understanding page experience

Blocked — needs the owner’s imagery decision (S-18) · VisualD9-05

Three routes exist and they need different permissions: keep labelled generic art (no new permissions), authorized Amazon-served or API imagery if API access is ever established, or original photography of owned units provided by the owner. The default stands until someone chooses.

Adds to the design prompt: “Use labelled generic art until authorized exact-product assets exist; when real imagery arrives, label its source and date next to the listing facts it illustrates.”

What would change our mind: Unpublished while blocked; the decision record names the route before any image changes.

Basis: Amazon Associates Program Policies

Proposed — needs an internal-linking look first · Information architectureD9-06

Group the reading room by the decision a reader is facing

The six guides already answer a sequence — format, capacity, cleanup, functions, thermometer, hosting — but the index presents them as one flat grid. A visible grouping makes the hub structure the buyer can feel, not just the links the crawler sees.

Adds to the design prompt: “Group the reading room by the decision a reader is facing, not by product type alone, so every guide is one visible hop from its two neighbours.”

THE NEXT DESIGN BRIEF

A prompt worth building from.

This is a proposed design direction grounded in the limits above—not an instruction to treat missing evidence as confirmed.

Design Zone3 Kitchen as a warm, restrained kitchen publication, not an analytics dashboard. Lead with real buyer questions and a source-linked five-product shortlist: three air fryers and two meat thermometers. Use an ivory background, deep green typography, editorial serif headlines, generous spacing, purposeful diagrammatic art and accessible mobile navigation. Keep the Associates disclosure immediately below the masthead and repeated in the footer. Make every outbound Amazon product link sponsored and clearly label the Amazon destination. Keep all research dashboards behind one navigation group and accessible in the footer. Separate intended ASINs and confirmation fields from observed redirects. Never invent a reviewer, test result, price, rating, certification, popularity signal, order, conversion score or revenue forecast. Use labelled generic art until authorized exact-product assets exist. Build useful question-led guides with honest authorship, source dates, related links, canonical URLs, sitemap and RSS coverage. Preserve the single five-event privacy counter and require a durable collector before production activation. Keep the campaign ledger empty until authorized first-party evidence arrives. Suggestions must expose known facts, missing information, discovery steps, safe defaults and the evidence needed for a decision. Saving a review must not implement it. Publication, API content and account-level changes stay gated on authorization and evidence.

The publishing gate.

  1. DiscoverUse authorized first-party inputs. Keep provenance and capture date.
  2. DraftQuestion-led, source-linked copy. Missing facts stay absent.
  3. CheckDisclosures, sponsored links, intent, API permissions, unsupported claims, feed and sitemap.
  4. ApproveRequire real evidence and account authority for gated capabilities.
  5. MeasureUse real query, click and report evidence—not forecasts—to choose the next question.
