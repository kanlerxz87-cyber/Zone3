Amazon Associates rules and FTC disclosure obligations

Disclosure: as an Amazon Associate this site would earn from qualifying purchases. This disclosure is rendered on every page because both the Amazon Operating Agreement and the FTC endorsement rules require it to be hard to miss — see Compliance. Exact current wording: FLAG-12.

Disclosure what you must show, and where

Two separate obligations apply, from two separate authorities, and they are frequently collapsed into one sentence. They are not the same thing.

1. What Amazon requires

The Amazon Associates Operating Agreement requires a statement identifying you as an Amazon Associate to appear clearly and conspicuously on your site. The Associates FAQ gives the operative wording as:

"As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."

This site renders a disclosure banner at the top of every single page, before any product data, because that is what "clearly and conspicuously" means in practice.

2. What the FTC requires

The FTC requires you to disclose any material connection — a financial, employment, personal or family relationship — with a brand. Its guidance is specific about placement: the disclosure must be hard to miss, placed with the endorsement itself, in simple language, and not buried at the end of a post, on an About page, behind a "more" link, or mixed into a group of hashtags.

The FTC also prohibits talking about your experience with a product you have not tried, and prohibits claims a product's maker could not substantiate.

The three that block revenue

Two of these are policy violations, not polish. Both are cheap to fix and neither can be automated away.

  1. FLAG-01 — hand-captured prices. Amazon permits a site to display prices only if Amazon serves the link showing them, or if the price came from Creators API or PA API. Every price in this project was read by hand. Either move to an API-fed or Amazon-served price, or remove the price columns before the site goes live.
  2. FLAG-05 — no Associates tag. Without a tag parameter in the URL there is no Special Link and no commission, full stop.
  3. FLAG-02 — live promotion badges. All three products carried a live promotion at capture. Once the promotion ends, the reference must come down. On a static page you cannot guarantee that, so the safe default is to drop the badge column.

Jurisdiction the operator is in the EEA

The Associates country selector lists the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, France, Japan, Canada, Italy, Spain, India, Brazil, Mexico, Australia, UAE, Singapore, Netherlands, Saudi Arabia, Sweden, Poland, Belgium, Ireland, Turkey and Egypt. Norway is not on it. All product data in this project is from Amazon.com (US).

Separately, the Operating Agreement puts the burden on you to satisfy GDPR (EU) 2016/679 and the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC where they apply, including disclosing your use of cookies, pixels and similar technology through a privacy policy. A site that targets EEA visitors needs a consent layer. This project has no privacy page yet and sets no tracking cookies.
