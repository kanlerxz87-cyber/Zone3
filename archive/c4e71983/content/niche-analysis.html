Keyword and content direction editorial judgement, explicitly not measured data

No search-volume figures appear anywhere on this page, on purpose. No official source publishes keyword volume for Amazon or Google search. Any number you see quoted for "air fryer" search volume on a third-party blog is that vendor's estimate. Inventing one here would be indistinguishable from a measured figure, so the columns that would hold it are empty.

What can be said from the official data that was captured:

  • Intent clusters are visible in the titles. The official product titles themselves tell you what Amazon's sellers are optimising for: capacity ("6 Quart", "26QT", "8 Qt"), function count ("4-in-1", "24 cooking functions", "9-in-1"), and feature hooks ("Sync Finish", "Hi-Fry", "rotisserie"). These are the exact phrases a buyer sees, and they come from an official source.
  • The comparison structure is pre-built. Three products, three tiers, one category. "Versus" content between them requires no additional product research.
  • Sub-category pages map to distinct intents. Dual basket, toaster oven and large capacity are three different jobs to be done, each with its own page.

Once the site is live, real query data arrives from Google Search Console and from Amazon's own reports. Until then, treat keyword priority as a hypothesis to test, not as data.
