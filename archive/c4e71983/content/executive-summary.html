Executive summary the whole project in one page

Objective. Build an AI-assisted Amazon affiliate operation around three supplied product links: automate product research, identify and analyse the niche, model commission economics, generate SEO content, manage affiliate links, and track performance — while staying inside the Amazon Associates Operating Agreement and FTC disclosure rules.

What the links turned out to be. All three short links were fetched and followed. Each returns an official Amazon.com product page. All three products are air fryers, in the same Amazon department (Home & Kitchen) and the same browse path (Kitchen & Dining > Small Appliances > Fryers > Air Fryers).

What that means. The niche found you. Three products, one category, three different price and capacity tiers within it. That is a coherent starting catalogue rather than three unrelated items, and it supports a tight content plan: one category hub, one page per sub-category, comparison articles across the three tiers, and buying guides per use case.

Strategic read. The three products sit at roughly $80, $80 and $150, against a category whose official best-seller list is led by products at $89.99, $99.99 and $39.99. Two of the three are mid-market; the Emeril Lagasse unit is the premium anchor. A single-category site with a good, a better and a best tier is the classic structure that converts, and the data supports that structure here.

The published standard rate for the Kitchen category is 4.50%. Amazon lists Home separately at 3.00%, and does not publish which category it assigns to a given ASIN, so both cases are shown. At the Kitchen rate a qualifying sale of the Emeril Lagasse unit is worth about $6.75; the two Chefman units are worth about $3.60 each. That spread is the single most important commercial fact in this summary: the premium unit is worth nearly twice as much per conversion, so it deserves the most prominent placement even though the cheaper units will convert more often.

Prime bounty events pay $12.00 per successful free-trial or paid membership sign-up. On a $79.93 air fryer at 4.50%, a Prime sign-up is worth more than three air fryer sales. Any content plan that ignores Prime is leaving money on the table.

Recommended sequence what to do, in order

  1. Decide the marketplace. All data here is from Amazon.com, but the operator is in Norway and Norway is not in the Associates country list. Pick US or a European store before doing anything else, because it determines the commission schedule and the tax schedule (FLAG-06).
  2. Join Amazon Associates and get a tag. Without a tag in the URL, nothing earns (FLAG-05). Then use the Link Builder to produce compliant Special Links.
  3. Write the original content. Amazon rejects sites without it (FLAG-13). A data table is not original content in Amazon's sense.
  4. Fix the price display. Replace hand-captured prices with an Amazon-served widget or a Creators API / PA API feed, or delete the price columns (FLAG-01).
  5. Add a privacy and cookie page. Required by the Operating Agreement and by GDPR / ePrivacy for an EEA-targeting site (FLAG-06).
  6. Get three qualifying sales inside 180 days or the application is withdrawn (FLAG-12).
  7. Only then automate. Build the collector, the content pipeline and the reporting layer described on the Automation Architecture page.

Bottom line

The product research is done and verified, and the niche is confirmed and coherent. The economics are thin per unit — $3.60 to $6.75 at the published Kitchen rate — so this only works with volume, with Prime bounties, or by moving traffic toward the $149.99 unit. Nothing can earn yet: there is no Associates tag, no original content, and the price display as it stands breaks the Program Policies. Those three items are cheap to fix and are the whole gap between this repository and a monetised site.
