Why there are no numbers here

The brief asks the platform to track clicks and conversions and to optimise campaigns continuously. There is no Associates account, no tag in any link, and no traffic. There is therefore no click data, no conversion data and no earnings data.

The tempting move is to ship the dashboard with zeros, or with illustrative figures. Both are wrong: a zero looks like a measurement, and an illustrative figure looks like a measurement too. Once a number sits in a table with a metric name beside it, a reader cannot tell it apart from a real one. Every cell on this page therefore reads no data yet - pre-launch.

What will populate it, and from where

The source of truth for clicks, conversions and earnings is Associates Central. Amazon's own reporting is the record it pays against, so it is the only sensible input for financial metrics. A self-hosted click logger is useful for on-page behaviour — which link on which page got clicked — but it must never be treated as a substitute for Amazon's conversion numbers.

Guardrails for when tracking is switched on

  • Sub-tags, not user identifiers. Amazon allows sub-tags to compare placements but forbids associating any sub-tag with a specific end user of your site. Do not assign sub-tags dynamically per visitor.
  • Consent comes first. The Operating Agreement puts the burden on you to disclose your use of cookies, pixels and similar technologies, and to satisfy GDPR and the ePrivacy Directive where they apply. An EEA-facing site needs a consent layer before any analytics or tracking fires — FLAG-06.
  • Never let automation touch your own links. Bot or automated activity through your own Special Links is a termination risk — FLAG-17.
  • Amazon may audit. The Operating Agreement reserves the right to monitor, record and disclose information about your site and to crawl it for compliance verification.
