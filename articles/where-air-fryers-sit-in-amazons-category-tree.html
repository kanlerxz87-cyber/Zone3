Where air fryers sit in Amazon's category tree - and why the category decides the commission

One breadcrumb, one row of a public rate table: how the 4.50% Kitchen rate attaches to the three listings this site tracks, and when the money actually arrives.

The short version

  • Commission rates on Amazon Associates are set per category, and all three listings this site tracks sit in the Air Fryers category, inside Kitchen.
  • Amazon's public Standard Commission Income Statement puts Kitchen at 4.50% - but every account has its own schedule, so no per-product rate is claimed anywhere on this site.
  • The three clocks that matter after a sale: about 60 days after month end for payment, and the balance thresholds before anything is paid at all.

Read the breadcrumb, not the product name

A product page's category is printed right on the page, as a breadcrumb above the buy box. For the three listings this site tracks, all three breadcrumbs read the same way: Home & Kitchen > Kitchen & Dining > Small Appliances > Fryers > Air Fryers S14 S15 S16. Amazon's Best Sellers page for the category is published at the same browse node, 17659096011 S43.

The category matters because Amazon's commission structure is category-based. The Standard Commission Income Statement's Table 1 fixes rates by product category - and Kitchen is one of the named rows, at 4.50% S25. 'All other categories' default to 4.00% S25. That is the whole mechanism: the breadcrumb decides the row, the row decides the rate.

The rate table is public; your rate card is not

Two different documents carry commission rates, and confusing them is a common mistake. Table 1 of the Standard Commission Income Statement is public - it is the category table quoted above S25. The account-specific commission schedule, by contrast, is published to each Associate's own account and can differ S03.

That is why every per-product commission cell on this site is blank. The public table says what the standard rate is for a category; only the account's own schedule says what a specific account earns on a specific sale. Publishing a per-product rate without the account schedule would be exactly the kind of unsupported figure this site exists to avoid.

There is also a separate programme for Onsite content with its own table S26 - one more reason a single 'the commission is X%' sentence is usually wrong somewhere.

When the money actually arrives

Amazon's policies state that commission income is paid approximately 60 days after the end of the month in which it was earned S02. Before anything is paid at all, the balance must clear a minimum: for the US marketplace, a USD 10 minimum for deposit and gift-card payouts, and USD 100 for a cheque S02.

Two more timing rules shape expectations. A new application is reviewed after the applicant has driven at least three qualifying sales within 180 days, and personal orders do not count S29. And an account that has not referred a sale within 180 days can be closed under the dormancy rule S28.

For the newer Creators API - the data interface that would let a compliant site display live prices and ratings - the prerequisite is higher: at least 10 qualifying sales within the past 30 days S04.

What a reader should take from this

If you browse this site's comparisons and click through to the listings, nothing about your experience changes - you land on Amazon's own page and shop normally. The category breadcrumb and the public rate table explain what the site's operator might earn on a qualifying purchase: 4.50% of the sale at the standard Kitchen rate, on the account's own schedule, paid on Amazon's timing S25 S02.
