The 24-hour window: how Amazon's shopping session actually pays

24 hours, 89 days, 180 days - the three clocks inside every qualifying purchase, read from the Commission Income Statement, plus the things that quietly cancel a commission.

The short version

  • A click starts a shopping session that lasts 24 hours - unless the reader buys, or clicks someone else's affiliate link first.
  • Something left in the cart can still pay out for up to 89 days after the original click, provided the order completes, ships and is paid for within 180 days.
  • Returns, cancellations and refunded orders are stripped from commission - and purchases that arrive through a 'Redirecting Link' can be disqualified outright.

Clock one: the 24-hour session

The Commission Income Statement defines a Session as beginning "when a customer clicks through that Special Link" and ending "upon the first to occur of the following: (x) 24 hours elapse from that click, (y) the customer places an order for a Product, other than a digital product..., or (z) the customer clicks through a Special Link to an Amazon Site that is not your Special Link" S02.

Clause (z) is the one most people miss: the session is exclusive. If your reader clicks your link today and a different site's affiliate link tonight, the second click ends your session - and whatever they buy afterwards is credited to the other site, not you S02.

There is no extension for 'engagement'. The window is the window, which is why affiliate copy that gives a reader a reason to act now is not just conversion craft - it is arithmetic.

Clock two: the 89-day cart

A product added to the cart during the session gets its own, longer clock: the purchase can complete "no later than 89 days after their initial click-through of the Special Link" S02. That covers the very common pattern of a reader who clicks, carts, and then sleeps on a big-ticket purchase - the air-fryer-oven-at-christmas pattern.

The 89-day clock is tied to completing the order for that Product, not to browsing generally. It is a cart window, not a cookies-everywhere window.

Clock three: the 180-day ship-and-pay

Even a completed order is not paid until it ships and settles: within 180 days of the purchase, the Product must be "shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer" S02. Pre-orders, backorders and slow fulfilment all live inside this rule.

After that, the accounting clock: commission income is paid approximately 60 days after the end of the month in which it was earned, and only once the balance clears the marketplace minimum - in the US, USD 10 for deposit or gift card, USD 100 for a cheque S02.

What cancels a commission

Three cancellations are written directly into the policies. Returned, refunded or cancelled orders are disqualified S02. Purchases made through a Redirecting Link - a link that sends the reader indirectly to Amazon - are disqualified S30. And the session rules themselves disqualify credit whenever the last affiliate click before purchase was someone else's S02.

For a content site, the Redirecting Link clause is the one to engineer around deliberately: it is the reason this project refuses to wrap product links in intermediaries while the submitted links' own status is unresolved (see 'What an Amazon affiliate link has to look like' on this desk).

Why this site cannot show you its conversion numbers yet

Everything above describes how commission works in principle. What it does not do is produce numbers for this site: no Associates tag is connected, so no clicks and no conversions exist to report. Amazon's own reporting is the only compliant source for those figures when they exist, and Amazon's policies prohibit generating artificial clicks or sessions to manufacture them S02.

Until the account inputs arrive (open questions Q1 and Q4 on the Decision Center), the campaigns ledger on this site stays deliberately empty - which is the honest state of a site that has not yet earned anything.
