Principles

  1. One voice, one palette, one grid. If a section needs to feel different, it gets a different accent inside the same system, not a new system.
  2. Lead with the product and the reader's question. Evidence is a supporting block, never the opening argument.
  3. Every number on the page is dated and sourced, and every gap is stated in one plain sentence instead of being filled.
  4. Photography is real or absent. There is no third option.
  5. The page should answer one question completely and then get out of the way.

One design system

Type

  • Display: One serif for headlines only (Iowan Old Style / Palatino / Georgia stack, already in use). Sizes: 40/32/26/22 px on a 1.08-1.2 line height.
  • Body: One sans for everything else (system stack, already in use). 16.5 px body, 1.65 line height, 68-74 character measure.
  • Weights: Two only: regular and 650. No light, no black.
  • Rule: No letter-spacing on body text. Uppercase only on 12 px eyebrows, at 0.16em.

Colour

  • Ink and paper: #14181f on #ffffff paper, with a warm off-white #faf7f0 for page wash
  • Accents: One deep green #0b6b53 for actions and active states, one warm copper #b35a2a for prices and dated stamps only
  • Surfaces: One border colour #e6e2d8, one shadow: 0 10px 30px -14px rgba(15,23,42,.30)
  • Semantics: Green for verified, amber for unverified, red for blocked. Used as chips and left borders, never as page backgrounds.
  • Rule: No gradients behind text. Gradients are allowed only inside a hero band with a solid overlay.

Grid

  • 1180 px content, 22 px gutters, 8 px spacing scale (8/16/24/40/64)
  • repeat(auto-fit, minmax(268px, 1fr)) with 12-16 px gaps
  • 14 px cards, 10 px buttons, 8 px chips. One radius family.
  • Single column below 720 px, tap targets 44 px minimum, sticky comparison bar on comparison pages only.

Imagery

  • Amazon-served image URLs or photographs taken by the owner. Nothing generated, nothing copied from a search result.
  • 4:3 product cards, 16:9 hero, 1:1 avatars. Every image has alt text that names the product.
  • Any owner photograph carries a caption with the date it was taken.

Copy rules

  1. Lead with the answer, not with the context. The first sentence of any page should be usable on its own.
  2. One concrete fact per paragraph. A paragraph with no specific number, name or date is a paragraph to cut.
  3. Write to one reader. 'You' is allowed; 'users', 'consumers' and 'individuals' are not.
  4. Every claim about a product is either quoted from a listing with a date, or absent.
  5. No claim of hands-on experience. This project has not handled any of these products, and saying otherwise is an FTC problem, not a style problem.
  6. No popularity claim without a dated source. 'Best seller' is only allowed with the date it was observed and a link to the page.
  7. Say what is missing in one sentence, plainly. A gap stated honestly reads as competence; a gap papered over reads as generated.

Anti-slop checklist

  1. Does the page open with a specific question a real person would type?
  2. Is there at least one number or name in the first two sentences?
  3. Is every image either Amazon-served or an owner photograph with a date?
  4. Does every outbound button name its destination?
  5. Is the disclosure visible without scrolling?
  6. Is there a one-sentence statement of what could not be verified?
  7. Are there more than two different page backgrounds on this page? (Should be no.)
  8. Does any sentence appear verbatim on more than three other pages?
  9. Is there a byline or an author note with a date?
  10. Would the page still be useful if every adjective were deleted?

What not to do

  • Do not add a star-rating widget before API access exists.
  • Do not invent testimonials, reviewer quotes or reader questions.
  • Do not generate product photography or lifestyle scenes.
  • Do not put a countdown, a stock counter or a 'trending now' badge on the page: there is no data behind it, and a fake urgency signal is the fastest way to lose a reader.
  • Do not hide the outbound destination behind a shortened URL or an unlabelled button.
  • Do not build a fifth visual language for a new section.

The prompt, ready to paste into a design tool

Everything above, written as a single brief. It names the hard constraints first so a generated design cannot quietly break a rule.

You are designing the visual and editorial system for a small Amazon Associates publication that reviews and compares kitchen appliances and tools.

WHAT IT IS
A reader-facing shop and guide site. Five products: three air fryers and two meat thermometers. Each product page must lead with the product, state what the Amazon listing actually says with a date and a link, and state plainly what could not be verified. Evidence is a supporting block, never the headline.

HARD CONSTRAINTS (these come from Amazon's Operating Agreement and Program Policies, and cannot be designed around)
1. The sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." must be clearly and prominently visible on every page, above the fold, and repeated in the footer.
2. No price and no star rating may be displayed unless Amazon serves the link that shows it, or it comes from the Creators API. Design the product card to look complete without a price: the commercial element is a labelled button that names the destination, for example "See today's price on Amazon".
3. Every outbound link must make it obvious that it goes to Amazon, and carries rel="sponsored".
4. No generated or stock-styled product photography: images are Amazon-served URLs or photographs the owner took, with a date caption.
5. Every page must contain original commentary, analysis or transformation, not a restatement of listing copy.

THE LOOK
Editorial, calm, physical. Warm off-white paper, near-black ink, one deep green accent for actions, one warm copper accent reserved for dated stamps and prices. One serif for headlines, one sans for body, two weights only. 14px card radius, one shadow, one border colour. 1180px content width, an 8px spacing scale, generous white space, no decorative gradients behind text. It should look like a well-made independent kitchen publication, not a template and not a dashboard.

THE PAGES
1. Front page: masthead, disclosure strip, one hero question, five product cards with real imagery, three latest guides, two category hubs, a three-line dated evidence strip, footer with the audit links tucked into one group.
2. Category hub: question-shaped headline, two-sentence answer, a three-question chooser, product cards, comparison entry point, dated evidence box.
3. Product page: image beside a specification block, "who this is for" and "who this is not for", what the listing states with date and link, what could not be verified, labelled outbound button.
4. Comparison: attribute table, sticky header on mobile, blanks marked unverified rather than filled, method note with dates.
5. Guide: the question as the headline, the answer inside the first sixty words, concrete specifics throughout, dated evidence box.

THE WRITING
Short sentences. One concrete fact per paragraph. No claims of hands-on experience: nobody here has used these products. Banned: "in today's fast-paced world", "when it comes to", "look no further", "game-changer", "elevate your cooking", "let's dive in", "seamlessly", "cutting-edge", "carefully curated", and any popularity claim without a dated source. State what is missing in one plain sentence instead of filling it.

WHAT IT MUST NOT LOOK LIKE
Not a dashboard: no KPI tiles, no status callouts, no source-count statistics, no site map in the reader's path. Not a template: no emoji bullets, no gradient hero with three floating cards, no generic illustrated characters. Not AI-generated: no symmetrical three-column feature grid with icons, no "why choose us" section, no invented testimonials.

DELIVER
A single design system in CSS custom properties, the five page templates as HTML with real copy for one air fryer and one thermometer, a type and colour scale, and a one-page written specification that a developer can build from without asking questions.
