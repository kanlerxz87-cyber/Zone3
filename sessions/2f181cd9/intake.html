Why the two sides are never merged. An intended ASIN is a claim by the owner about what they meant to submit. An observed destination is what a live link served on a particular date. Copying one into the other would invent a fact, so the intended columns stay empty until the owner fills them, and the observed columns carry their own fetch date. Amazon defines an ASIN as a ten-character identifier (S82); all five submitted identifiers are nine characters, and the direct amazon.com/dp/<identifier> URL returns Page Not Found for every one of them (S74 S75).
