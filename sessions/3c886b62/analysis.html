Reviews

Reviews

What was captured

For each product, Amazon publishes an aggregate star value and an approximate ratings count on its search-results listing. Those were captured for all three:

  • CHEFMAN 6 quart dual basket: 4.2 out of 5 on 4.8K ratings.
  • Emeril Lagasse 26QT: 4.3 out of 5 on 18.3K ratings.
  • Chefman 8 quart TurboFry: 4.4 out of 5 on 16.8K ratings.

Amazon displays those counts rounded to one decimal place with a K suffix, so they are approximate, not exact integers.

What could not be captured

Amazon's star histogram, the distribution of five-star through one-star ratings, and the review text itself are served via JavaScript and behind sign-in. None of it could be read from an official static page, so none of it is reported here. That means the sentiment analysis the brief asks for is only partly satisfied: volume and average score are captured, themes and complaints are not.

A constraint worth stating plainly

Associates Program Participation Requirements section 6(t) prohibits displaying Amazon's customer reviews or star ratings on your site unless they were obtained through the Creators API or the Product Advertising API. The values above were not. They are published here as internal analysis, and they must be re-sourced through one of those APIs before this becomes a live Associates property.

What can still be concluded

The spread is narrow, 0.2 stars across the whole set, so none of the three is distinguished by quality perception. What does differ is the size of the evidence base. 18.3K and 16.8K ratings are large enough that the average is stable; 4.8K is a materially thinner sample. Combined with the purchase counts, where two products run at 3K+ per month and one at 1K+, the review data supports treating the two higher-volume units as the safer recommendations.

Competition

Competition

Inside the set

The three products compete directly with each other. They share a niche, a department, and in two cases a brand: two of the three are Chefman products, at 6 quarts and 8 quarts, priced within two cents of each other. That is a genuine cannibalisation risk for anyone writing about both. A reader choosing between the CHEFMAN 6 quart dual basket and the Chefman 8 quart TurboFry is choosing between two baskets and one large basket at essentially the same price, and the 8 quart has roughly three times the review base and three times the recent purchase volume.

Outside the set

No wider competitor set was captured, and that is a deliberate limit rather than an oversight. The brief supplied three links, and the rule for this project is that every value must come from an official source. Pulling in the wider air fryer market would have meant quoting price trackers or review blogs, which is exactly what the verification rule forbids. The honest position is that this analysis covers three products, not a market.

How to widen it properly

There are two compliant routes. The first is Amazon's own Best Sellers listing for the air fryer category, which is an official Amazon page and would give a real rank-ordered competitor set. The second is the Product Advertising API, which returns browse-node data and would let the competitor set be rebuilt on a schedule. Both are better than scraping, and the API route is the only one that also fixes the price and rating display problem at the same time.

What the price competition looks like

Every one of the three has a cheaper used or new offer sitting below the Amazon price: $57.45 against $79.93, $49.54 against $79.91, and $92.99 against $149.99. That is the competitive pressure that matters most here. A reviewer who sends traffic to a listing where a third-party seller is cheaper is sending traffic that may not convert at the featured price, and the commission is calculated on what Amazon actually receives, not on the headline figure.
