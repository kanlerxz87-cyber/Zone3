### Inside the set

The three products compete directly with each other. They share a niche, a department, and in two cases
a brand: two of the three are Chefman products, at 6 quarts and 8 quarts, priced within two cents of each
other. That is a genuine cannibalisation risk for anyone writing about both. A reader choosing between
the CHEFMAN 6 quart dual basket and the Chefman 8 quart TurboFry is choosing between two baskets and one
large basket at essentially the same price, and the 8 quart has roughly three times the review base and
three times the recent purchase volume.

### Outside the set

No wider competitor set was captured, and that is a deliberate limit rather than an oversight. The brief
supplied three links, and the rule for this project is that every value must come from an official source.
Pulling in the wider air fryer market would have meant quoting price trackers or review blogs, which is
exactly what the verification rule forbids. The honest position is that this analysis covers three
products, not a market.

### How to widen it properly

There are two compliant routes. The first is Amazon's own Best Sellers listing for the air fryer
category, which is an official Amazon page and would give a real rank-ordered competitor set. The second
is the Product Advertising API, which returns browse-node data and would let the competitor set be
rebuilt on a schedule. Both are better than scraping, and the API route is the only one that also fixes
the price and rating display problem at the same time.

### What the price competition looks like

Every one of the three has a cheaper used or new offer sitting below the Amazon price: $57.45 against
$79.93, $49.54 against $79.91, and $92.99 against $149.99. That is the competitive pressure that matters
most here. A reviewer who sends traffic to a listing where a third-party seller is cheaper is sending
traffic that may not convert at the featured price, and the commission is calculated on what Amazon
actually receives, not on the headline figure.
