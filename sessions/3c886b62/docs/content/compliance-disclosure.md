There are two separate obligations here, and they are worth keeping apart because they come from
different places and bite at different times.

### What Amazon requires

Amazon, *Associates Program Participation Requirements*, section 3(b), makes the Associate solely
responsible for the site's compliance with all governmental requirements:

> including those related to disclosures (for example, if applicable, the U.S. FTC Guides Concerning the
> Use of Endorsement and Testimonials in Advertising) and electronic marketing, data protection and
> privacy…

So Amazon's contract points at the FTC rule rather than restating it. Breaching it is also a breach of the
Operating Agreement, because any violation of the Participation Requirements is deemed a material breach.

Separately, section 4 makes the Associate the *Sender* of every communication containing a Special Link
for the purposes of marketing law, including the CAN-SPAM Act and the Telephone Consumer Protection Act.

### What the FTC requires

From the FTC's *Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers*:

- Disclose when you have any **financial, employment, personal, or family relationship** with a brand. A
financial relationship is not limited to money; if you got anything of value to mention a product,
disclose it.

- Make disclosures even if you think your evaluations are unbiased, and do not assume your followers
already know.

- Place the disclosure so it is **hard to miss**, and place it with the endorsement message itself. It will
be missed if it appears only on an About page, at the end of a post, or behind a *more* link.

- Use **simple and clear** language. Terms like *advertisement*, *ad* and *sponsored* are fine. Avoid vague
or confusing terms such as *sp*, *spon*, *collab*, or a bare *thanks*.

- Do not mix the disclosure into a group of hashtags or links.

- In a video, put the disclosure in the video, ideally in both audio and video, not only in the
description.

- It is your responsibility. Do not rely on a platform's disclosure tool.

- You cannot talk about your experience with a product you have not tried, and you cannot claim a product
is terrific if you were paid to talk about it and thought it was terrible.

### What this site does

Because no Amazon Associates tag is configured, there is no financial relationship to disclose today: no
commission can accrue. The site states that in a banner on every page rather than displaying an affiliate
disclosure that would be inaccurate.

When a tag is added, the standard Amazon disclosure wording, *As an Amazon Associate I earn from
qualifying purchases*, must go into that banner and into the body of any page carrying a tagged link,
placed where it is hard to miss and not only in the footer or on an About page. That change is tracked as
F07 on the Flagged for Review page.

### Two more things worth flagging

The FTC requires that you cannot describe experience with a product you have not tried. Every claim on
this site is drawn from Amazon's own published product data, not from hands-on testing, and the pages say
so.

Amazon also prohibits making inaccurate, overbroad, deceptive or otherwise misleading claims about any
product, price or promotion. The two most likely ways to trip that here are quoting a stale price, which
is why every price is dated, and repeating a promotion badge after the promotion ends, which is tracked
separately as F08.
