Why the intent fields and the observation fields are never merged

A redirect target is an observation: it is what Amazon served when the link was followed, on the dates recorded, and anyone can reproduce it. An intended product is an instruction: it exists in the mind of whoever submitted the link, and no official page states it. Merging the two fields would turn the observation into an assumption and then into marketing copy — the exact move that makes a site untrustworthy. The split also has a practical use: if Amazon ever changes where a short link lands, the observation field changes and the drift is visible, while the intent field stays whatever the owner said it was. The three listings sit in Amazon's browse path Home & Kitchen › Kitchen & Dining › Small Appliances › Fryers › Air Fryers, shown on each listing page I2-S10 I2-S11 I2-S12.

What would close the gaps

  • The intended ASINs. The account owner states, in the coordination log, which ASIN each submitted link was meant to promote — or confirms the observed destinations. Either answer fills the blank column with a sourced statement. Q2
  • The Associates tag. Until one exists, no link on this site is a Special Link and no buy action is active; every Amazon link is an evidence link marked rel="sponsored". The tag is question Q1; the no-tag finding is I2-F-03.
  • The Special Links test, one product at a time. Once the account is enrolled and a tag exists: build one tagged link, click it from a real visit, confirm the destination and that the click appears in the Associates reports, and only then scale to the rest. Until the account holder runs that test, no claim is made here about any link's validity or compliance status — the submitted short links are recorded as observed, not judged. I2-S02
