What "4-in-1" actually names — and what a listing leaves out

Every appliance category has these counted claims: 4-in-1, 24 functions, 9-in-1. They are the fastest way to compare machines and the easiest way to be misled, because the number and the list are not always the same thing. Here is how to read three of them without taking anything on trust.

Why the temperature is the one number worth checking

Maximum temperature is the rare spec that is stated, comparable and meaningful without a test. On the same day, in the same Amazon category, other listings publish theirs plainly: the Ninja Air Fryer Pro line reads "1750W, 400F max, Fits 4 lbs fries", and the Cosori TurboBlaze line reads "90°-450°F, 5 Fan Speeds … 1725W". Against those, a "450°F Hi-Fry Option" is a stated top end at the higher of the two figures — and "for crispier results" is the listing's own claim about what that buys you, not ours. CBA-S06

That matters for one specific job: frozen food. A higher dry-heat ceiling is what "frozen fries, crispier" marketing is usually describing. Whether you get it is a function of load, basket size and how much you shake — none of which this page can tell you, because nobody here cooked in these machines.

A two-minute way to check any counted claim

  1. Find the enumeration. A "4-in-1" that names four modes is a spec. A "4-in-1" that names three is a name. Search the title and the "About this item" bullets for the list before you search for the number.
  2. Separate counts from capabilities. "24 presets" tells you about the control panel, not about what the machine can do that yours cannot. Ask which three you would use on a Tuesday.
  3. Look for the units. A temperature with "°F", a capacity with "QT", a power with "W" are checkable. "Advanced", "smart" and "perfect" are not.
  4. Check the same claim across two pages. The listing title, the bullets and the comparison table in a listing do not always agree. Where they differ, the number you can see twice is the one worth quoting.

What this page will not claim

That any of these machines is "versatile enough to replace your oven". Nobody here cooked in them, and the FTC's guidance is that you cannot describe an experience with a product you have not tried — which is exactly the sort of sentence that makes a review site read like it was generated. CBA-S12

Which machine has more functions. One line says "4-in-1", another says "24 one-touch preset options". Those are not the same unit of measure, and a table that lined them up as if they were would be a made-up number.
