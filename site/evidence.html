This page exists because “verified” is a word affiliate sites use loosely. Below is every official page this site read, what it said, and the dated result of checking each claim against it.

How a page gets read

  • One page, one fetch, one record of what it said
  • No value taken from a search snippet
  • A sign-in wall or a block is recorded as a result
  • The agent identifies itself, as Amazon's Agent Terms require

Agent Terms §4(c) require an Agent/[name] user agent and forbid circumventing a block. I2-S02

Why the disclosure appears twice

Two different obligations, two different sources. Amazon's Operating Agreement §5 requires the exact sentence “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” somewhere prominent on the site. The FTC's guidance goes further on placement: a disclosure is likely to be missed if it appears only in a footer or behind a click, and it should sit with the recommendation itself. So this site does both — the exact Amazon sentence in a banner at the top, and a plain-language line beside any action that could lead to a purchase. I2-S01 I2-S06

Why a blank cell is a feature

Three rules do most of the work here. Each is quoted below as the page carries it.

  1. Price. Program Policies 2(b): “You may only show prices and availability if: (a) we serve the link in which that price and availability data are displayed, or (b) you obtain Product pricing and availability data via Creators API or PA API”. I2-S02
  2. Star ratings and reviews. Program Policies 6(t): “You will not display or otherwise use any of our customer reviews or star ratings … unless you have obtained a link to that customer review or star rating through Creators API or PA API”. I2-S02
  3. No invented experience. FTC: “You can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried” and “You can't make up claims about a product that would require proof the advertiser doesn't have”. I2-S06
