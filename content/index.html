Why the inventory is five rows of "Not generated"

An article about `B04G1MEwY` could only be written by inventing what the product is. Amazon's own rule forbids that: "You must not make inaccurate, overbroad, deceptive or otherwise misleading claims about any Product". The correct output of the content stage under an empty evidence set is an empty content set plus a flag — not fluent prose.

The generation contract, stated precisely

Every sentence in a generated article is bound to a named evidence field. If the field is absent, the sentence is not written. In practice the generator emits a page whose specification looks like this, with blanks where data is missing, and a flag per blank:

{ "asin": "<10-char>", "title": "ItemInfo.Title", # verbatim, never rewritten "specs": ["ItemInfo.ByLineInfo[*]", "ProductInfo.TechnicalInfo[*]"], "price": {"field": "OffersV2.MiniPriceListings[0].Price", "stamp": "<iso8601>", "lowest_used": "<required for comparisons>"}, "rating": "CustomerReviews.StarRating + .Count", "claims": [ {"text": "...", "evidence": "ProductInfo.FeatureBulletPoints[2]"} ], "must_include": ["associate_disclosure", "amazon_content_disclaimer", "price_disclaimer"], "forbidden": ["review_bodies", "non_amazon_prices", "superlatives_without_evidence", "implied_endorsement", "expired_promotions"] }

The two most common failure modes this contract prevents are fluent speculation (a model asserting a 5-quart capacity because air fryers usually have one) and stale certainty (a price or in-stock claim presented as current when it is a month old). Both are explicitly banned by the no-misleading-claims rule, and both are invisible to a reader — which is why they are enforced in the renderer rather than left to a style guide.
