What the decision states mean

do-now

Recommended for the first pass. Cheap, unblocked, and removes a visible defect.

do-next

Recommended, but it depends on a decision or a design pass.

blocked

Cannot be built until the named question is answered. The blocker is a policy gate, not a preference.

defer

Worth doing later. Doing it now costs more than it returns.

do-not-do

Proposed and rejected. The policy reason is recorded on the row.

Priority P1/P2/P3 is sequencing, not importance. Effort S/M/L is a rough sizing against a single focused pass. Risk is the compliance or trust exposure if the item is done badly.

How to run the review

  1. Start with the questions, not the proposals. Anything marked blocked cannot be built well, however good the design is.
  2. Answer the three critical questions first: the tag (Q1), the intended products (Q2), and account standing (Q4). Everything else is downstream of those.
  3. Work the do-now column next. Those items are cheap, unblocked, and remove the defects that make the site read as machine output.
  4. Record accept, reject or defer against every row before writing any code, and record the person who decided.
  5. Only then run the design brief's prompt against the accepted scope.
  6. Re-run the release gate after every change. A page that cannot trace a claim to an official source does not ship.
